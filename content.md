Please provide any background information regarding your organization/business that you would like included.
Wilderness Medicine Outfitters was founded by Carl Weil, a 3rd-generation guide, who began teaching advanced first aid/backcountry care to ski patrollers in 1967 at Colorado State University. In 2006 he became the first non-physician to earn the "Fellow" designation from the Academy of Wilderness Medicine (educational arm of the 3,000+ member Wilderness Medical Society), and in 2012 became the first to complete the Master Fellow degree. Since 2012, every WMO WFR/WEMT class has included at least one Fellow of the Academy of Wilderness Medicine as an instructor — a distinction WMO states no other school matches. Carl's background includes Nationally Registered EMT, EMT-IV/MAST, Colorado Instructor-Coordinator, CPR Instructor Trainer, and National Wilderness Instructor Trainer credentials; work as a Trip Medic (including a Mt. Everest expedition); experience in Denver General's Emergency Department; founding a rural ambulance service; over 3,000 outdoor business risk-management reviews; and service as an expert witness in 300+ legal cases. WMO is a founding member of Professional Outdoor Medical Educators (POME) and has been featured on TV (including Fox) and referenced in National Geographic.

What does a typical "job" look like for your organization/business?
A typical class is either a 9-day in-person intensive (roughly 8am–6pm daily, about 1/3 lecture, 1/3 Q&A/demonstration, 1/3 hands-on practice) held at WMO's rural Elizabeth, CO property, or a 6-week blended format with two evening classroom sessions per week (6–10pm) plus three weekend field days (8am–5:30pm), combining online lessons with in-person or Zoom skills sessions. Classes are capped at 24 students. WMO also travels to teach at client locations/organizations upon request.
